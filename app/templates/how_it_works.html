How JusticeGram Works

A technical breakdown of our multi-stage legal classification, statutory retrieval, and matching architecture.

1
Input Ingestion & Preprocessing

When a user enters a problem in English or Hinglish, the raw text undergoes normalization. This includes URL stripping, punctuation cleanup, lowercasing, and domain-specific stop word filtering (removing both English conversational fillers and common Hinglish stopwords such as "kya", "mera", "ho gaya", "batao").

2
Ambiguity & Safety Detection

Rather than hallucinating or confidently guessing on overly brief inputs (such as "need lawyer" or "problem with money"), the system checks input length, unique token counts, and vague keyword signatures. If ambiguous, it tags the query as Other / Needs Clarification and presents targeted clarifying prompts to guide the user.

3
TF-IDF Vectorization & Logistic Regression

Clean text is transformed into a high-dimensional feature vector using TfidfVectorizer with unigram and bigram extraction (ngram_range=(1, 2)) and sublinear term frequency scaling. A multinomial LogisticRegression classifier with balanced class weights evaluates the feature vector and generates a probability distribution across the 15 Indian legal categories, outputting a primary category and a confidence percentage.

4
Keyword Heuristic Cross-Check & Subcategory Detection

If model confidence falls below a 35% threshold, or if serialized model artifacts are unavailable, JusticeGram seamlessly switches to a rule-based domain keyword heuristic engine. It simultaneously detects fine-grained dispute subcategories (e.g. UPI fraud, marksheet retention, wrongful termination, security deposit) and explains why the classification occurred.

5
Statutory Retrieval & Lawyer Match Explanations

Based on the predicted category and subcategory, JusticeGram retrieves structured guidance from our verified legal knowledge base:

  • Relevant Indian Laws: Attributable statutory frameworks (BNS, BNSS, BSA, Consumer Protection Act, IT Act, PoSH, RERA) with direct India Code links and verification dates.
  • Document Checklist: Concrete physical and digital evidence the citizen should organize.
  • Procedural Steps: Plain-language guidance on notices, pre-institution mediation, or complaint lodgement.
  • Advocate Matching with Explanations: Matches demonstration advocates and details transparent reasons for the match (specialization, location, pro bono status).
  • Free Legal Aid: Direct access to NALSA/DLSA clinics and national helplines (1930, 1915, 1091, 15100).
